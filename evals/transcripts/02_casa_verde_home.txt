Discovery call: Casa Verde Home
Date: 2026-09-16 | Duration: 28 min
Attendees: Sofia Ramirez (Founder and CEO, Casa Verde), Tom Becker (E-commerce Manager, Casa Verde), Javier (PM)

Javier: Tell me a bit about Casa Verde and how the online store runs today.

Sofia: We sell sustainable home goods, furniture, planters, textiles. About 60 employees, most of them in the warehouse. The store is on Shopify and we launch around 80 new products every month.

Tom: And every one of those products needs a description, SEO title, meta description, alt text for the images, and then the same thing in Spanish for the Mexico store. I have one copywriter. She is completely underwater.

Javier: How long does one product take today?

Tom: Around 40 minutes if it's simple, more if it's furniture with dimensions and materials. We have a backlog of about 200 products that are live with a placeholder description. Those pages barely convert.

Sofia: That backlog is money sitting on the table. Our conversion on pages with a real description is about double the ones with the placeholder.

Javier: Where does the product information come from?

Tom: The supplier sends a spec sheet, usually a PDF or an Excel file. Materials, dimensions, care instructions. Then our copywriter rewrites it in our brand voice. We have a brand voice guide, it's a Google Doc, but nobody really follows it consistently.

Sofia: The voice matters to me. We're warm, a bit playful, and we never use greenwashing language. We got called out on Instagram once for saying a product was eco-friendly when only the packaging was.

Javier: So accuracy on sustainability claims is a hard requirement.

Sofia: Absolutely. Anything that says recycled, organic, or carbon neutral has to be backed by the supplier sheet.

Tom: The Spanish store is the other headache. We use a translation plugin and it's awful. Things like "throw pillow" come out as a literal translation. Customers in Mexico message us confused.

Javier: How do you know which product pages are underperforming?

Tom: Shopify analytics, but I look at it maybe once a month. There's no process.

Javier: Who would approve new tooling?

Sofia: Me, and I'd want Tom to own it. Budget is tight, we're a small team, so it has to pay back fast. If it clears the backlog in a month, that alone justifies it.

Javier: If we did one thing first?

Tom: Clear the 200 product backlog with descriptions that sound like us and don't make claims we can't back up.
